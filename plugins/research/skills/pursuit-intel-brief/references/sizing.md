# Sizing a pursuit against comparable work

No fees, margins or client outcomes are kept in this file: the plugin is published publicly. Gather
the comparables fresh each run from the Digital SOW approval channel and the relevant pursuit
channels, and keep them in the run's `03-sizing.md` checkpoint, never in this skill.

## What to capture per comparable

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Engagement and post date | Use the date of the approval post, not the start date. |
| Fee and contract type | Fixed fee, T&M, capacity program, or a mix across SOWs. Note any rate schedule the client required. |
| Duration | Weeks. For multi-SOW programs, give each SOW's length. |
| GP | As posted. Note if it's a range or changed between review and signature. |
| Team shape | Roles and FTE or hours per week. Note onshore/offshore split. |
| Outcome | Won, lost, cut, renegotiated, still open. Include what the client pushed back on and what we gave. |

Pick the three to six closest by scope (strategy vs build vs run), client type and size. Use one
large non-comparable program as a ceiling if the pursuit could grow into a build phase, and label
it as a ceiling.

## Run-rate math

1. For each strategy-shaped comparable, divide fee by weeks to get a per-week run rate.
2. Take the range across comparables, then multiply by the working deck's timeline.
3. Adjust up for scope breadth the comparables didn't carry (multiple workstreams, a technology
   execution plan, a business case, a district as well as a venue) and for roles they didn't staff
   (data/AI architect, business-case analyst). Say what you adjusted for.
4. Present the result as a range next to the Notion estimate and the deck's own number. If they
   disagree, say so; the deal review will ask.

If there's no timeline yet (common at RFI stage), assume one, say so, and label the range as an
assumption.

## Deal review rules

Look up the current GP floor, the approval needed below it, and the triggers for a deal review in
the deal review channel or its pinned posts, and quote them with the post date. They change;
don't rely on memory. Put them in the brief next to the sizing so the team sees which review
this deal will need.

## Structuring patterns worth checking

- Fixed fee against milestones is the usual shape for strategy; T&M for pods and run work.
- A pod model with small/medium/large sizing lets a client say yes at a lower number without
  re-scoping.
- A Phase 0 on an LOI, or a preliminary win, covers a kickoff date the contract can't meet.
- Some public and education clients require a cooperative purchasing rate schedule; check before
  pricing.
- If comparable clients negotiated the price down, build the concession into the structure
  (phases, pods, optional scope) rather than the margin.
- Agency-style RFPs that ask for "agency fees" and "estimated costs of the project" as separate
  lines expect a fixed strategy fee plus a capital estimate. Keep the two unmistakably distinct.
