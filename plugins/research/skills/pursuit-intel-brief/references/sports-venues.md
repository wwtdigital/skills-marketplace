# Checks for stadium, team and league pursuits

Load this when the client is a team, league, venue or district developer.

## Incumbents to look for first

The surprises in a venue pursuit are usually vendors the team hasn't named. Search the client's press releases and local business press for:

- An "Official IT Infrastructure Partner" or "Official Technology Partner" (often a regional VAR; check whether it has since been acquired).
- The connectivity sponsor (carrier or cable company) and whether the deal covers the new building.
- Ticketing, facial entry, screening, POS/concessions, loyalty and mobile app vendors.
- Hospitality/commercial partners (Legends, Oak View Group, Elevate, Aramark, Levy) and what mandates they hold; their staff may be embedded on the client's roster.
- The AV/acoustics designer, the technology designer (ME Engineers is common), the owner's rep (CAA ICON is common), the architect and CM.

- The owner's-side delivery team: project manager or PM alliance (Brailsford & Dunlavey, CAA ICON, McKissack), builder JV, and whoever is packaging technology RFPs. These firms often decide the tech procurement path.
- Sponsors who already hold the lanes WWT wants: an "Official Intelligence/AI Partner," a cybersecurity partner covering the parent group, a data/research agency. Check the club's partner page and recent press.

Read the client's own front-office roster page. It lists partner staff, the IT team by name, and analytics leads. Compare against the buyer map.

## The usual venue tech pattern

Recent NFL builds follow a shape: a carrier as connectivity founding partner, a named networking OEM (Cisco or Extreme), a separate technology designer, an owner's rep running RFPs, a neutral-host DAS owned by either the carrier or the owner. Where WWT fits is integration across those parties plus the data/identity/AI layer. Say which of those roles are filled and which are open.

## Benchmark venues (public sources, as of Sep 2026)

- Bills, Highmark (2026): Cisco Wi-Fi 7 converged network, Verizon-owned DAS, standalone tech building, CEIA screening. Opening complaints about views, screens and concession lines.
- Titans, Nissan (2027): Extreme Wi-Fi 7 + MatSing, ANDREW digital DAS, Verizon, ME Engineers, in-house SVP Tech.
- Intuit Dome (2024): identity-led build by Globant over ~22 months; opening-night facial-auth failure.
- SoFi / Allegiant (2020): Cisco Wi-Fi 6 + Cisco Vision / Cox founding partner.
- Refresh this list each run from public sources: which peer venues opened recently, and which are still early in tech procurement.

## Landmine themes

Check the team's do-not-say list, then verify their own deck against it. Recurring sensitivities in venue pursuits: relocation and the old site, seat licenses, public funding, the supporter section's culture and pricing, transit that isn't funded, the quarterback or coaching situation, economic-impact figures the client publishes, and dynamic/surge pricing.

## Reads from renderings and site plans

Look at posted renderings for: how much of the journey is surface parking (arrival tech scope), roof material and daylight (video-board brightness, RF behavior), airport or highway adjacency (FAA, drones, RF coordination), 365-day plazas (identity that can't depend on ticketing), and where the supporter section sits. Mark all of these as your inferences.

## Client appendix tells

When the RFP includes the client's own strategy document, look for: internal contradictions (a "mandate" on one page and "optional" on another), named preferred vendors, operating targets (staff ratios, training hours, team sizes) that give the operating-model workstream real numbers, and requests the client has already made that match what you're proposing (a command center, an identity layer). Quote those back rather than proposing them cold.
